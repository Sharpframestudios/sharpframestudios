01What Framer Is, and Why We Build In It

Framer is a design tool and a website platform in one product. You lay a site out on a canvas the way you would in a design file, and that canvas is the site — no separate step where a design is handed to a developer and reinterpreted. Framer describes itself as covering the path "from idea to launch," with hosting, security, analytics, CMS and SEO built in rather than assembled from plugins.

Sharp Frame Studios is a one-person studio in Sarasota. Strategy, design, build, photography and film are handled by the same person — no handoffs, no account managers, no stock imagery. Framer suits that because it removes the seam where small projects lose their quality: the gap between what was designed and what got built.

Framer expertise sits alongside premium visual design, conversion-focused builds and in-house photo and video. Those are related: shooting the photography for the layout, rather than sourcing images to fit one afterward, is much of why the finished pages hold together.

02How It Compares to What You Are Probably Weighing

Most local businesses that call us weigh four options. The honest shape of each:

WordPress is the most flexible and the most demanding. It runs almost any plugin, and that is the liability. WordPress's own security documentation is direct: hosts are "responsible for the infrastructure on which your website sits," not the application, and the owner must keep core, themes and plugins updated. Fine with an in-house marketer. A standing obligation for a contractor who wants a site that stays fixed.

Squarespace is the low-effort option and genuinely competent. The constraint is the ceiling: a site built on an opinionated template tends to look like one, and in a market with Ringling College in it, that shows.

Webflow is the closest comparison — a deeper structural model, often the better call for a complex marketing site with many interlocking content types. Framer's advantage is iteration speed and motion; Webflow's is granular control over markup and CSS.

A hand-coded build gives the best long-term ceiling and costs the most to build and change. Right when the site is really an application; wrong when it is eight pages that need to look excellent and stay editable.

We cover making this decision without a vendor steering it in our guide to web design in Sarasota, and the signs a rebuild is warranted in 12 signs your business has outgrown its site.

03What a Framer Build Includes

Every project is scoped individually, but the spine is consistent:

  • Positioning and page structure decided before anything is designed — what the site must get someone to do
  • Custom layout on the canvas, mobile first, with type, spacing and motion handled as part of the design
  • Photography and video shot for the layout, so hero, card crops and detail shots come from one shoot
  • CMS collections where content repeats — projects, services, locations, posts
  • Forms, tappable phone numbers, maps and whatever booking tool the business already runs on
  • Titles, descriptions, headings, URL structure, redirects from the old site and a sitemap
  • A walkthrough at handover so the owner can make routine edits without calling anyone

The CMS is what most often decides whether a site ages well. Collections hold structured content with text, rich text, image, date, reference and file fields, import from CSV or Google Sheets, and generate detail pages from one template — so adding a project is a content task, not a design task.

04Speed, SEO and Maintenance

Framer sites are hosted by Framer, on AWS across a globally distributed edge network with a worldwide CDN, with HTTP/3, TLS 1.3, certificates renewed automatically and DDoS protection on all sites. Connecting a domain you already own is a DNS change — two A records and a CNAME at your registrar.

Pages are pre-rendered rather than built in the browser on every visit, using static site generation and Traffic-aware Pre-Rendering, with images compressed at the edge to WebP and AVIF. Worth knowing for search: crawlers "fall back to the latest available optimized version, never the unoptimized one."

None of that makes a site fast on its own — a 6 MB hero image is slow on any host. The targets worth holding a build to are Google's Core Web Vitals: largest contentful paint within 2.5 seconds, interaction to next paint at or under 200 milliseconds, cumulative layout shift at or under 0.1. Achievable in Framer with disciplined images; not automatic.

The SEO tooling is unremarkable in the good sense: auto-generated sitemaps and robots.txt, indexing controls, redirects, canonical URLs, Open Graph images and JSON-LD. None of it earns rankings by itself — that comes from the work in our local search strategy guide.

Maintenance is the quiet advantage: no server to patch, no plugin stack to keep compatible. The trade is that you rent a platform rather than own a stack.

06When Framer Is the Wrong Tool

We will say so rather than take the project. Framer is a poor fit when:

  • The site is large and content-heavy. Framer's plan limits cap pages per site, and a publication, directory or knowledge base with thousands of URLs runs into the edges of the model.
  • You need a real store. Framer has no native commerce backend. Ecommerce comes from marketplace plugins such as Framer Commerce, which syncs products from Shopify and acts as a design layer over it. Good if you want Shopify anyway; otherwise start with the store platform.
  • There is a custom back-end — member accounts, quoting engines, booking logic with real business rules — that is application work, and belongs in a hand-coded build.
  • You need to own and host the code — Framer is hosted, so a compliance requirement to run your own infrastructure rules it out.
  • Your team is already deep in WordPress: people who know it, workflows built on it, no complaints — migrating is cost without benefit.

Choosing the platform is a smaller decision than most vendors make it sound. What makes a site work is almost always positioning, structure, photography and clarity — a point we argue in why your website is still your most important digital asset.

07Questions We Get Asked

Can I edit the site myself?

Yes. Text, images and CMS entries are editable without touching the design, and we walk you through it at handover. Structural changes remain design work.

What happens to my current rankings?

Existing URLs are mapped and redirected as part of the build, so old links keep working.

Do I have to move my domain?

No. You keep it where it is and point DNS at Framer.

Can you shoot the photography too?

Yes — photo and film are produced in house for the layout they are going into, not licensed from a library.

Do you work outside Sarasota?

We work across the region and remotely beyond it. Our Lakewood Ranch guide and Venice guide cover those markets specifically.
