01Who This Is For

Sharp Frame Studios is an independent studio in Sarasota, Florida. Strategy, website design, development, photography and film are handled by one person, start to finish. There are no handoffs, no account managers and no stock imagery.

That setup suits a particular kind of client: a local business that wants a website built around what it actually sells, made by someone who has seen the space, met the team and understands the offer. Restaurants, contractors, medical practices, marine services, galleries, event vendors and professional firms all fit. A company that needs six stakeholders in a weekly status call does not.

Sarasota is also a market with an unusually calibrated eye. The arts calendar, the hospitality sector and the design school raise the floor for what reads as a current, credible business. The practical consequences are covered in more depth in our guide to what local businesses need to win online in 2026, but they come down to three things: say what you do in plain language, show real work rather than borrowed photography, and make the next step obvious on a phone.

The local layer matters too. A Sarasota business is rarely only a Sarasota business. If you also serve Siesta Key, Lakewood Ranch or Bradenton, the site should say so, because a homeowner outside the city limits will assume the answer is no unless it does.

  • Business Website, from $3,000. Up to five custom pages, forms, a portfolio, services, menu or gallery section, a basic CMS where required, and Google Analytics and Search Console connected.

A single landing page with your own photos and finished copy sits near the bottom. A ten-page site with a shoot, a CMS and a content system sits near the top.

05One Person Instead of an Agency or a Template

There are three common ways to get a website, and they fail differently.

A template is cheap and fast, and it will look like what it is. It also tends to be structured around the demo content rather than your business, so the page you most need often has nowhere to go. It is a reasonable choice for a business that has no photography, no copy and no clear offer yet.

An agency brings depth and a bigger team, which is genuinely useful above a certain scale. Below that scale you are frequently paying for coordination. The person who understood your business in the pitch meeting is not always the person writing the copy, and the brief loses something at each handoff.

A single-operator studio removes the handoffs. The person who sat down with you writes the structure, designs the pages, builds them, shoots the photography and sets up the SEO. The tradeoff is honest: one person has finite capacity and takes on a limited number of projects at a time. In exchange, nothing gets lost in translation and there is one person to ask.

06Questions

How long does a website take?

Most projects go from strategy to launch within several weeks. Page count and content readiness set the pace more than anything else.

Can I update the site myself?

Yes. A basic CMS is included where a project requires it, and the Signature tier supports up to two CMS collections plus a blog or case-study system. Routine edits like hours, prices and new projects should not require a developer.

Do I need new photography?

Not always, but it is usually the single highest-return line item. Photography and video are handled in house and shot to the layout, which is different from commissioning a beautiful set of images that do not fit the places the design needs them.

Do I need a full rebuild or just a refresh?

Sometimes a site is structurally fine and needs new content and photography. Sometimes it is working against you. The clearest way to tell is to read through our list of twelve signs a business has outgrown its site and see how many apply.

Will the site rank?

No one can ethically promise a position. What a well-built site can do is make the business easier for both people and search engines to understand: titles, headings, URL structure, internal links, page speed and accurate business information, all handled during the build. That is where the probability comes from.

Is a website still worth it when most of my customers find me on social?

The website is the one asset you own outright, and the argument is laid out in why your website is still your most important digital asset. Social platforms rent you an audience on terms they can change.

How do we start?

Send a short note about the business, what the site needs to do and roughly when you want it live. You will get a reply within one business day, and the first conversation is about fit and scope rather than a proposal.
