Halloween Social Media Ideas for Local Businesses

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01Why Halloween Is Worth a Little Effort

Halloween falls on a Saturday this year, October 31, which gives local businesses a full weekend to work with. According to a September 22, 2026 press release from the National Retail Federation, based on a survey of 7,889 consumers conducted by Prosper Insights & Analytics, 74 percent of Americans plan to celebrate Halloween, and total spending is expected to reach $13.5 billion. The same release puts expected spending at $115.14 per person.

Most of that money goes to candy, costumes and decorations, so not every business will see a direct sales lift. A tax preparer or a roofer has little reason to sell anything spooky. But a holiday that most of your customers are already thinking about is an easy reason to post something friendly and timely, and that is the real value for a small business.

The goal is not to go viral. It is to remind people that you exist, show the human side of the business, and give them a reason to stop scrolling for two seconds. This post covers ideas that fit a small team, a short schedule and a modest budget, plus a few cautions so a fun idea does not turn into a headache.

02Start With What You Already Have

The easiest Halloween content is a normal day at your business with a small seasonal twist. You do not need new equipment or a script. You need a phone, decent light and about twenty minutes.

Here are some starting points that work for most local businesses:

  • Decorate one corner of the counter, front desk or truck and post a short video of it coming together.
  • Put the team in costume for a group photo, with the understanding that anyone who does not want to be on camera can skip it.
  • Show the pumpkin carving or door decorating contest in your own office, with a simple poll in Stories asking followers to vote.
  • Post a plain notice of your Halloween hours, especially if you close early on Saturday for trick-or-treating.

A plumber in Bradenton could post a photo of the crew van with a paper bat on the dashboard and a line about the weekend schedule. A hair salon in Venice could show a stylist doing a costume-inspired updo. Neither takes a campaign, and both feel like the business behind them.

03Ideas by Type of Business

Restaurants, cafes and bars have the most obvious material. A themed drink, a pumpkin dessert or a costume night gives you something to photograph. If you need help with the photos themselves, our guide to food photography on a phone covers light, angles and plating.

Retail shops can build a window display and post a short walk-up video. A boutique in downtown Sarasota might set out a basket of candy for families walking past, then post the basket and the hours. A gift shop might feature a few products that work as small trick-or-treat alternatives or party favors.

Service businesses have to be a little more creative, and that is fine. A landscaper can post a pumpkin and mum planting idea for front porches. A cleaning service can share a short, honest checklist for tidying up after a party. A pet groomer can post a costume photo contest for dogs, since costumes for pets are common enough that the NRF release lists pumpkin, hot dog and ghost among the top pet choices.

Professional offices, such as dentists, accountants and law firms, should keep things light and brief. A staff costume photo or a short note about candy and teeth, written in the office’s normal voice, is enough. If you work in a regulated field, keep your normal review process for anything you publish.

04Contests and Giveaways: Keep Them Simple and Legal

A costume contest or giveaway is a natural idea, and it is also where small businesses run into rules. Facebook and Instagram both expect you to run the promotion yourself and make clear that the platform is not involved. According to Woobox, a promotion software company summarizing the platforms’ guidelines, Facebook promotions should include a statement that Facebook is not involved, and Instagram accounts should have an official rules page that can be linked from the bio.

Woobox also notes that users should not be asked to tag themselves in photos they are not actually in. A safer approach is to ask people to comment, or to share a photo of their own costume. Woobox is a marketing guide, not the platform itself, so read Meta’s current promotion rules before you post.

Florida adds its own rule for larger prizes. According to the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services, an operator of a game promotion with a total announced prize value greater than $5,000 must establish a trust account, obtain a surety bond or submit a waiver, under section 849.094 of the Florida Statutes. A basket of candy or a gift card is far below that, but if you are thinking about a bigger prize, ask a lawyer first. We are not lawyers, and this is not legal advice.

For most small businesses, the simplest version is a free costume photo contest with a small prize, clear dates, a plain description of how the winner is chosen, and no purchase required. Announce the winner publicly on the date you promised.

05A Simple Plan for the Week Before Halloween

If you have only a few days, a short plan beats a long list of ideas. Pick two or three posts and put them on the calendar before you start shooting.

One workable schedule looks like this:

  • A few days before: post the decorations or team photo, with your Halloween hours in the caption.
  • Two days before: share one fun, useful or free thing, such as a costume contest, a seasonal special or a simple tip.
  • Halloween day: post a short Story or photo from the business and a thank-you to customers.
  • The day after: share the contest winner or a photo recap, and then go back to your normal posting.

If you are not sure how often to post in general, our guide on how often a business should post goes platform by platform. A holiday does not need to change your usual rhythm much, and a few extra posts for one week is plenty.

Use hashtags and a location tag, such as your town or neighborhood, so nearby people can find the post. Keep the caption short and in your own voice. Reply to comments quickly, because the replies often matter more than the post.

06What to Avoid and How to Measure It

Costumes and humor can go wrong. Avoid costumes that mock a culture, a group of people or a real tragedy, and skip jokes that could embarrass a customer or an employee. If a staff member is uncomfortable with a photo, leave it out. Also be careful with children: get a parent’s permission before posting a photo of someone else’s child.

Pay attention to timing and tone in a hurricane-prone area. If a storm is in the forecast during the last week of October, a lighthearted post can look out of touch, and customers will want weather and closure information first. Check your plans against the news before you publish.

To judge whether it worked, look at simple things: how many people commented, how many messages or calls came in that week, and whether anyone mentioned the post in person. No one can promise that a holiday post will bring in business or reach a certain audience, and platforms change how they show posts without notice. Treat it as a low-cost way to stay visible, not a sales campaign.
