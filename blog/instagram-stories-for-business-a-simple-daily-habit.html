Instagram Stories for Business: A Simple Daily Habit

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01The Short Answer, and Why It Is Not a Number

The question usually arrives as a request for a number: how often should a business post Instagram Stories? Once a day, three times, ten? The honest answer is that a small business does better with a habit than with a quota. One or two Stories on most days you are open, made in a few minutes from whatever is already happening, will serve a shop in downtown Sarasota or a contractor in Bradenton better than a burst of fifteen followed by three silent weeks.

Stories suit that kind of habit because of how they work. Instagram’s own Stories page describes them as photos and videos that disappear after 24 hours, and says you can share them with all your followers or only with your Close Friends list. Nothing stays on your profile grid afterward, so a slightly crooked photo of this morning’s delivery costs you nothing tomorrow.

That short life is also the weakness. A Story about your holiday hours is gone the next day unless you choose to keep it. Keeping the useful ones is the job of Highlights, which the second half of this post covers.

02What the Published Numbers Say

There is no official quota. In December 2024 the music industry blog RouteNote summarized a video from Adam Mosseri, the head of Instagram, about posting frequency in general. As RouteNote reported it, he said that posting often helps with reach, that no one solution suits every account, and that the better choice is a schedule that feels sustainable.

Companies that sell social media tools do offer numbers. Hootsuite’s guide to posting frequency, published in April 2025, recommends posting Instagram Stories two times per day. Plenty of small accounts post far less than that. Socialinsider, a social media analytics company, studied Stories from brand accounts between January and May 2025 and reported that brands with 1,000 to 5,000 followers posted about 12 Stories a month, roughly three a week, while brands with 100,000 to 1 million followers posted about 80 a month.

More is not automatically better. In May 2024 a Hootsuite writer ran a two-week test on her own business account: 15 Stories in the first week, at one or two a day, and 55 in the second week, at five to ten a day. Total reach rose in the heavier week, but the average reach of each Story fell from 131.1 to 103.2, and average engagement fell from 2.47 percent to 1.71 percent. That is one account over two weeks, so read it as an anecdote and not as a rule.

The Socialinsider study also looked at where viewers leave. It reported an exit rate of 23.8 percent on the first Story in a sequence, falling to around 12.5 percent by the fifteenth. In plain terms, the first frame is where you lose the most people, and those who stay tend to keep watching. If you post several Stories in a row, put the clearest one first.

Taken together, these sources point to a modest target for a local business: one or two Stories on the days you are open, and a few more when something is really happening. Our platform by platform guide to posting frequency covers feed posts and the other networks.

03What to Share: Five Kinds of Story

The hard part of a daily habit is not the tapping. It is deciding what to post while a customer is waiting at the counter. A short list of categories takes the decision away, because on any given day at least one of them is already in front of you.

  • Today. Whatever is new or different: the fish that came in this morning, the last two seats in Saturday’s class, the crew starting a roof in Palmer Ranch.
  • Proof. Finished work, such as a before and after, a plated dish, or an order boxed and ready for pickup.
  • Answers. One question you were asked this week, answered in a single frame. Do you take walk-ins? Is there parking behind the building? Do you travel to Venice?
  • People. Who is working today, a new hire, the owner at the register. Ask first, every time.
  • Notices. A change in hours, a sold-out item, a storm closure, road work in front of the entrance.

Stories can also ask something back. Instagram’s Stories page names the Add Yours, Polls and Question stickers as ways to get a response from viewers. A poll asking which of two specials should return next week gives a customer a reason to tap and gives you a small piece of real information. Use these when you actually want the answer, not as decoration.

On the Gulf Coast the audience changes with the calendar. When seasonal residents return in the fall, a retiree back in Lakewood Ranch wants to know whether you still close on Mondays and whether the person who helped them last winter still works there. During hurricane season, a Story is one of the fastest ways to say that you are closed or that you have reopened. Post the same notice on your website and your Google Business Profile, because not every customer uses Instagram.

04A Ten-Minute Daily Habit

A habit holds when it is attached to something you already do. Pick one fixed moment in the working day, such as unlocking the door, loading the truck or finishing lunch prep, and make that the Story moment. The whole routine looks like this:

  • Take one photo or one short video at the moment you chose.
  • Add a line of text that says what it is and why it matters today. Do not rely on sound alone, since someone watching in a waiting room may have it off.
  • Post it and put the phone away.
  • Later in the day, look at the replies and answer them. Instagram’s Stories page notes that viewers can like a Story or reply to it by direct message, and a reply from a customer deserves the same attention as a phone call.
  • Once a week, check who watched. Instagram says you can see who has seen your story for up to 48 hours after you post it.

A loose weekly pattern helps on the days when nothing stands out: people on Monday, an answer on Tuesday, finished work on Wednesday, and so on. Hold it loosely. If the week’s best moment arrives on a Thursday, post it on Thursday.

If you miss a day, nothing breaks. No one can guarantee how many people Instagram will show a Story to, in the same way that no one can guarantee a position on Google. What you control is whether the people who do look find something recent and true. When a Story clip turns out especially well, the same idea may be worth a proper video, and our post on Instagram Reels ideas for beginners explains how to start.

05Highlights: Keeping the Stories That Still Matter

Instagram’s Stories page says that Stories you add as highlights remain visible as highlights until you remove them, even after the original Story has disappeared. When Instagram introduced the feature in December 2017, its announcement said that Highlights appear in a section on your profile below your bio, and that Stories are saved automatically to a private archive that only you can see. The same announcement said auto-archiving can be turned off in settings, so it is worth checking that yours is switched on. The archive is what lets you add last week’s Story to a Highlight today.

Think of the daily Stories as the supply and the Highlights as the shelf. Once a week, look back through what you posted and ask one question: which of these would a new customer still want to see a month from now? Most will not qualify. The few that do go into a small set of Highlights like these:

  • Hours. Current hours, holiday changes and where to park.
  • Menu or Services. What you offer, in the simplest form you can manage.
  • Work. Before and after pairs, finished projects, popular dishes.
  • Questions. The Answers Stories from the list above, collected in one place.
  • Season. One Highlight that you empty and refill as the year turns, for winter hours, summer specials or storm updates.

Each Highlight gets a name and a cover photo when you create it, according to the steps on Instagram’s Stories page. Use one or two plain words for the name, so that a visitor can read the row at a glance, and pick a cover that looks like the thing inside.

Then keep the shelf tidy. Instagram’s 2017 announcement said you can have as many Highlights as you like, but four to six that are current will do more for you than fifteen that nobody maintains. Remove the Story with last year’s prices. Our guide to setting up Instagram for business covers creating the first Highlights as part of the profile itself.

06Where Stories Fit, and When to Do Less

Stories are a way of staying in touch with people who already follow you. They are not a replacement for a place where a stranger can find your services, your service area and a way to contact you. The two work best as a pair: the Hours Highlight should match the hours on your website, and the website should be where a Story sends someone who is ready to book.

If a daily Story is more than you can keep up, three a week with tidy Highlights is a respectable routine. By Socialinsider’s figures, that is roughly what brands with small followings post anyway. Posting less is better than posting filler, because a Story with nothing to say teaches people to skip the next one.
