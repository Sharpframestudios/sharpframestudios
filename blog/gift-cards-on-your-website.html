Gift Cards on Your Website

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01Why a Gift Card Belongs on Your Website

A gift card is one of the few products a customer can buy without visiting you, without knowing what size or flavor the recipient wants, and without waiting for stock. For a restaurant, a spa or a small shop, that makes it a natural thing to sell online, especially from November through December when people are searching for last-minute presents.

The problem is that many local businesses sell gift cards only at the counter. A customer who thinks of you while sitting on the couch, or a relative in another state who loved their visit last winter, has no way to buy one. A page on your website that sells a card in a couple of minutes closes that gap.

This post covers the main ways to do it, what the common platforms say about their own setup and fees, the Florida rule you should know before you print a single expiration date, and how to present the card so people actually find it. It makes no promise about how many cards any business will sell.

02Three Ways to Sell Gift Cards Online

Most small businesses end up in one of three setups, and the right one usually follows from what you already use to take payments.

  • Your point-of-sale system sells them. If you already run a system such as Toast or Square, the gift card feature is often an add-on that links to a buying page you place on your site. The cards you sell online and the cards you sell in person live in one place, which keeps the balances consistent.
  • Your online store sells them. If your site already has a shop, a gift card can be one more product. This suits retail and product businesses that already take orders online.
  • A separate gift card service sells them. Some businesses use a dedicated service and link to it from the site. This can help when your point of sale does not offer gift cards, but it adds one more account to manage.

None of these needs to be complicated, but each has terms that are easy to miss. The next section looks at what the platforms themselves say.

03What Square, Toast and Shopify Say About Their Gift Cards

These details change, so treat this as a snapshot of what each company’s own pages said in November 2026 and check the current terms before you commit.

According to Square’s gift card page, a business can offer digital or physical gift cards, or both. Square says eGift cards are free to set up and that there are no subscriptions or long-term commitments. It also says a standard processing fee applies when a customer buys a card, and that a load fee of up to 2.5 percent applies each time funds are added to a card. Square states that its gift cards do not expire.

According to Toast’s product page, restaurants can sell eGift cards directly on their website or through Toast Online Ordering. Toast’s setup article describes a gift card module that you add through Toast Shop, a shareable link where guests buy eGift cards, and a default limit of $500 per eGift transaction. It also says gift cards cannot be used on orders that come through the DoorDash, Grubhub or Uber Eats integrations. Toast’s pages did not state pricing for the module, so ask for current pricing before you decide. If you are comparing ordering systems more broadly, our post on restaurant online ordering covers that choice.

According to Shopify’s help center, a store owner can activate gift cards to sell gift card products and can also create gift cards directly from the admin. The same page says physical cards that work with Shopify POS can be ordered in supported countries. The page did not list which plans include gift cards or how recipients receive them, so confirm that on your own account. If you are weighing a shop at all, our post on boutique websites walks through the tradeoffs.

04The Florida Rule on Expiration Dates and Fees

This is the part that surprises people who have run a business in other states. According to Florida Statutes section 501.95, a gift certificate bought in Florida, which the statute defines to include gift cards and stored value cards, may not have an expiration date or expiration period. The same paragraph says it may not carry any postsale charge or fee, and it lists service charges, dormancy fees, account maintenance fees and cash-out fees as examples.

The statute allows a few narrow exceptions. These include certain charitable contributions, employee incentive programs, loyalty or promotional programs where the recipient pays nothing separate, and some limited-duration events such as conferences. A card sold to the public for a dinner or a facial does not fall under those, so a simple rule for most local businesses is that the card does not expire and carries no inactivity fee.

We are not lawyers, and this is not legal advice. Federal rules and your payment provider’s terms may also apply, so ask your attorney or accountant if you plan promotions, bundles or bonus cards. What matters for your website is that the wording on the gift card page should not promise an expiration date. Check that your point of sale is not set to add one by default.

05How to Present the Card So People Find It

A gift card that lives three clicks deep will not sell. Put a clear link in your main menu or on your homepage during the holiday weeks, and keep a quieter link in the footer all year. People search for this directly, so a page titled with your business name and the words gift cards is easy for them to recognize.

On the page itself, keep it short. Show what the card looks like, let the buyer pick an amount, and say how it arrives, such as by email or text message. Add a line that explains how to redeem it, whether that is at the table, at the front desk or online. A buyer who is unsure how the recipient will use the card is a buyer who leaves.

Here are a few examples of how the wording changes by business:

  • A restaurant can suggest amounts that match a dinner for two, and say whether the card works for takeout and dine-in.
  • A spa or salon can offer a set amount or a specific service, and explain how appointments are booked with the card.
  • A shop can offer a few set amounts and mention whether the card works online and in the store.

Spa and wellness businesses that make claims about results should also read our post on what to show and what not to claim, since a gift page is still advertising.

06Timing for the Season and the Details People Forget

If you want gift cards live for the holidays, set them up now rather than in the last week of December. Test the purchase yourself from a phone, send a card to a personal email address, and redeem it once. Many problems only show up in that last step.

On the Gulf Coast there is a second reason to plan early. Many seasonal residents and visiting family members buy for people who live here, and a card that can be bought from out of state is useful to them. Our post on getting ready for snowbird season covers other places to tidy up before the busy months.

Finally, decide who watches the inbox. Someone should receive the order notices, and a customer who never got the email should be able to reach you easily.
