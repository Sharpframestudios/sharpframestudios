Instagram Bio for a Business: A Formula and Examples

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01What a Bio Has to Do in 150 Characters

Your Instagram bio is the one piece of text almost every new visitor reads before deciding whether to follow, message or leave. It sits under your photo and name, and it has a tight limit. According to Replug, a company that sells link-in-bio tools, the bio allows up to 150 characters in total, counting letters, numbers, emojis, punctuation and spaces. Practical Ecommerce, in an article published in 2021 and updated in 2022, gives the same 150 figure and adds that emojis can count as two characters.

We could not open Instagram’s own help pages while researching this, so treat those limits as reported by third parties. The edit-profile screen in the app shows a live character counter, which is the quickest way to confirm the current rule for your account.

With so little room, the job of the bio is not to tell your story. It is to answer four questions fast: what do you do, where are you, why should I trust you, and what should I do next? That is the whole formula, and the rest of this post shows how to apply it with examples, a few pitfalls and a quick way to test the result before you save it.

02The Formula: What, Where, Proof, Action

Write the bio in this order, one short line for each part.

  • What: the plain description of what you sell or do. “Sourdough and pastries” is better than “Baking with love.” A stranger should know your category within two seconds.
  • Where: the town or the area you serve. For a Gulf Coast business that might be downtown Sarasota, Lakewood Ranch and Bradenton, or Venice. If you work at a location, add the days or hours people need.
  • Proof: one specific, true fact that builds trust, such as years in business, a license, a certification or “family-owned since 2014.” Skip claims you cannot back up and superlatives like “best in town.”
  • Action: one next step, such as order, book, call or get an estimate, pointing at the button or link below the bio.

Replug’s advice for business bios lines up with this: state clearly what you do, include contact information, use line breaks, and add a clear call to action such as “Shop now.” Practical Ecommerce makes a similar point, noting that many companies use the bio only to say what they offer when it can do more. Both are sources with something to sell, so read them as practical tips rather than rules.

03Use the Name Field Too

The name field is separate from the bio, and Replug reports a limit of 30 characters for it. Practical Ecommerce says the name field is searchable, which means people may have a better chance of finding you when it holds more than just your name. It suggests using that space for extra keywords.

A bakery in Sarasota could use the name “Palm Avenue Bakery Sarasota,” which is 27 characters and includes both the business and the town. A person typing the town and the kind of business they want has a reason to see it. We cannot promise that any wording will make your profile appear in search, and Instagram does not publish how it ranks results, but it costs nothing to make the name clear.

Keep the real business name first. A clever tagline in the name field can make your profile harder to recognize, and it can confuse people who are looking for you by name.

04Four Examples for Made-Up Businesses

These are invented businesses, written to show the formula. Each fits within 150 characters, including spaces. In the app, you can put each part on its own line instead of using periods.

  • A bakery: “Sourdough, pastries and coffee. Downtown Sarasota, Tue-Sun 7-2. Family-owned since 2014. Order ahead below.”
  • A lawn and landscape company: “Lawn and landscape care. Lakewood Ranch and Bradenton. Licensed and insured, 12 years. Request an estimate below.”
  • A fishing charter: “Half-day and full-day fishing charters out of Venice, FL. Family-run for 20 years. Book a date below.”
  • A boutique: “Women’s clothing and gifts. Downtown Bradenton. Open daily 10-6. New arrivals every week. Shop below.”

Notice what each one leaves out. There is no motto, no string of emojis and no list of every service. If you do not have a real proof point yet, use a plain fact such as your hours or your neighborhood and add the proof later. An honest, specific line beats a made-up one.

05The Link, the Category Label and the Buttons

The bio points at things that sit around it. Replug says business accounts can show a category label and add contact buttons for email, phone or directions, as well as action buttons such as Book Now or Order. Check what your own account offers in the edit-profile screen, because Instagram changes these options over time.

Both Replug and Practical Ecommerce describe the profile as allowing one clickable link, and they point to link-in-bio tools for sharing more than one destination. Instagram may have changed this since those articles were written, so look at the options in the app. Whatever you choose, send people to a page you control. For most businesses that is the website, a booking page or an ordering page.

That is also why the link matters more than it seems. A profile can tell people what you do, but the website holds your full menu, prices, hours and contact details in one place. Our post on whether you still need a website if you have Instagram and Google goes into that.

06Mistakes to Avoid, and Keeping It Current

Most weak bios fail in the same few ways, and all of them are easy to fix.

  • Starting with a slogan. A line such as “Making every day sweeter” takes up room that should say what you sell and where.
  • Using emojis in place of words. A few can add warmth, but a row of them hides the message and, as Practical Ecommerce notes, may use up more of the count than you expect.
  • Leaving out the location. A visitor in Bradenton who cannot tell whether you are nearby will usually not ask.
  • Listing a phone number and an email in the text when contact buttons or a link already handle it. Use the room for something only the bio can say.
  • Promising a result you cannot control, such as a certain number of customers or a place at the top of any list. Keep the language factual.

If you run more than one location, or serve a wide area, name the area rather than listing every town. “Sarasota and Manatee counties” is clear and uses fewer characters than six town names, and it leaves you room for the proof and the action.

A bio is also not a one-time task, and it is worth putting a reminder in your calendar to reread it every few months. Seasonal businesses should update it when hours change, such as when winter residents return in the fall, or after a storm when you reopen. A bio that says “open daily” when you are closed on Mondays creates exactly the kind of small frustration that sends people elsewhere.

Before you finish, read your bio on your phone as if you had never heard of the business. Ask whether you can tell what it sells, where it is and what to do next. Then ask a friend who is not in your industry to do the same. If either of you hesitates, shorten it.
