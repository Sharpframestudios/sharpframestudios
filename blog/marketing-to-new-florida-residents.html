Marketing to New Florida Residents

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01A Newcomer Needs Everything at Once

Someone who has just moved to Sarasota, Bradenton or Venice does not have one question. They have dozens, and most of them are urgent. They need a dentist, a hair stylist, a plumber who will answer the phone, an internet provider, a place to eat on the first night when the kitchen is still in boxes. They do not have a trusted list of local names yet, so they start with a search.

That is the opportunity for a local business, and it is a quiet one. A newcomer has no history with you and no reason to prefer a competitor. What they have is a short window in which they are choosing many providers quickly, and a strong habit of checking a phone for each one.

This post is about what those people tend to look for and what your website, Google Business Profile and social pages can do to be easy to choose. It does not promise any particular result. Nobody can guarantee a position on Google, and a post like this one cannot tell you how many newcomers will find you.

02How Many People Are Actually Moving Here

It helps to have a realistic picture, because the headlines are louder than the numbers. According to Sarasota Magazine, in a May 2026 article on migration, Florida ranked eighth among states for net domestic migration in 2025, a decline from earlier years. The same article lists the net domestic migrants added to Florida as 318,855 in 2021 to 2022 and 22,517 in 2024 to 2025. Growth is still happening, but the pandemic surge has cooled.

Locally the picture is similar. The same article reports that Sarasota County added 16,618 residents in 2021 to 2022 and about 11,000 in 2024 to 2025, and that Manatee County added 15,296 and about 8,400 in those same periods. Still, thousands of people a year are arriving in the two counties.

Where are they coming from? According to the Realtor Association of Sarasota and Manatee, in a January 2026 release based on Florida Realtors data about out-of-state driver license exchanges, New York was the top domestic source for both counties in 2025, with 1,426 exchanges in Sarasota County and 1,332 in Manatee County. New Jersey, Illinois and Pennsylvania followed in both. The release also reports more than 1,300 exchanges in Sarasota County from people coming from U.S. territories or foreign countries.

A caution on those figures: a license exchange counts people who swapped a license, not everyone who moved, and it leaves out people who exchange later or not at all. Treat the numbers as a sign of direction, not a head count. The practical point is that a large share of your newest neighbors are arriving from other states and countries, and they are not familiar with local names.

03What a Newcomer Searches For First

Nobody can read a newcomer’s mind, but the pattern is easy to picture from the situation. They search for the services a household needs in the first weeks, and they search from a phone, often while doing something else.

The list tends to include the following kinds of searches:

  • Health and personal care, such as a family doctor accepting new patients, a dentist, a veterinarian, a barber or a salon.
  • Home help, such as an air conditioning technician, a pest control company, a pool service, a landscaper or a plumber.
  • Everyday life, such as a grocery store, a pharmacy, a gym, a coffee shop, and restaurants that are open late or deliver.
  • Setup tasks, such as a mover, a storage unit, a handyman to hang shelves, or a company to clean before the furniture arrives.

Notice what these have in common. Most are services where the customer needs to trust a stranger quickly. A newcomer cannot ask the neighbor next door which roofer is honest, so they lean on what they can see: reviews, photos, a clear description and a way to reach you.

04What Your Website Needs to Say to Someone Who Does Not Know You

Start with the basics, because newcomers check them first. Your homepage should say what you do, where you do it and how to reach you, in the first screen on a phone. If your service area is Sarasota, Lakewood Ranch and Bradenton, say so in plain words instead of leaving a visitor to guess whether you will come to their new address.

Give each main service its own clear page, with a plain description of what is included and what a first visit looks like. Our post on whether to use one page per service explains how to decide. A newcomer comparing three pool companies will tend to favor the one whose page answers their question without a phone call.

Answer the questions that only an outsider asks. A person from the Northeast may not know that a Florida air conditioner runs most of the year, that hurricane season shapes how some home services are scheduled, or that many seasonal residents leave for the summer. A short, calm paragraph that explains how your service works here is useful to them, and it shows that you know the area.

Make the contact step simple. A visible phone number, a short form and your hours matter more than a clever design. A person standing in a half-unpacked kitchen will not fill in a long questionnaire.

Finally, keep the site current. If your hours change for the summer, or you close for a week after a storm, say so on the homepage. A newcomer who arrives at a closed door because the website was out of date is unlikely to try you a second time, and they have plenty of other choices within a few miles.

05Google Business Profile and Social Media for Newcomers

For many newcomers the first thing they see is not your website but your Google Business Profile in the map results. Keep your hours, address, phone number and categories accurate, add real photos of your location and work, and write a description that says plainly what you do. Our guide to Google Business Profile descriptions has examples you can adapt.

Reviews carry extra weight for a person who has no other way to judge you, so it is reasonable to ask happy customers to leave one. Reply to reviews politely and briefly, including the unfavorable ones. Never write reviews yourself or offer something in exchange for a good one, since the platforms have rules against it.

Social media plays a smaller but real role. Newcomers often join local Facebook groups and follow local accounts to learn the area. A steady page with recent photos tells them you are open and active. A short welcome post, a clear list of what you offer, and a few posts that show your neighborhood can help. Keep the tone friendly and avoid anything that reads as a pitch aimed at people who have just arrived.

06A Simple Plan You Can Start This Month

You do not need a campaign. You need a handful of things that are accurate and easy to find. Check your Google Business Profile this week. Read your homepage on a phone as if you had never heard of your business, and note every question it leaves open. Add the answers, starting with your service area and what a first appointment involves.

Then consider one page written for newcomers: a short guide to what you offer, how booking works and what people from out of state usually ask. It can live on your site and be linked from your profile and social pages. Revisit it each season, since hours, availability and offers change.
