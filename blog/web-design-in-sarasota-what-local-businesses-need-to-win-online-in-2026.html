Web Design in Sarasota: What Local Businesses Need to Win Online in 2026

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01A Small Market With Unusually High Visual Expectations

Sarasota is not a single-industry town. The county economic development office lists healthcare, manufacturing, information technology, insurance, hospitality and logistics among its target sectors, and the largest employers range from Sarasota Memorial Health Care System to the window and door manufacturer PGT Innovations and FCCI Insurance Group. Underneath those names sits a dense layer of small businesses: restaurants, galleries, contractors, medical practices, marine services, boutique retail, wedding and event vendors.

What makes web design in Sarasota different from a comparable town elsewhere is not the size of the market. It is the visual literacy of the audience. Ringling College of Art + Design is here. So is a year-round arts and performance calendar and a hospitality sector that invests seriously in how it presents itself.

Someone who has just walked St. Armands Circle or Main Street has a calibrated eye by the time they open your website on a phone. A site that would pass without comment in a quieter market can read as neglected here.

That does not mean a Sarasota website has to be elaborate or expensive. It means the floor for “this is a real, current business” sits higher than average, and the gap between a careful site and a dated one is more visible to the people you want.

02Say What You Do, for Whom, and Where — Above the Fold

The most common failure in local web design is not ugliness. It is vagueness. A homepage headline that could belong to any company in any city forces the visitor to do work, and on a phone most of them will not do it.

Name the service, not the feeling

“Custom Kitchen and Bath Remodeling for Sarasota and Siesta Key Homes” tells a visitor three things at once. “Where Craftsmanship Meets Vision” tells them nothing they can act on. The second line can exist as supporting text. It should not be the first thing on the page.

This matters more in Sarasota than in a one-industry town because so many local categories are crowded and adjacent. There are a lot of interior designers here, a lot of dental practices, a lot of boat charters. Specificity is how a visitor sorts you from the others in the first few seconds.

State the service area honestly

A Sarasota business is usually not only a Sarasota business. Plenty of firms work across the city, Siesta Key, Longboat Key, Osprey, Nokomis and up into Lakewood Ranch and Bradenton. Say which of those you actually cover, because a homeowner on Longboat Key will assume the answer is no unless the site says otherwise.

Be equally clear about what you do not do. Turning away a bad fit on the website is cheaper than turning them away on a phone call.

Make the next step visible without scrolling

A tappable phone number, a short form, a booking link or a menu — one primary action, repeated consistently. Different businesses need different primary actions, and the design should be built around the one that matters, not around five that compete.

03Designing for a Town That Has Two Populations

Sarasota effectively runs two businesses a year. From late fall through spring the city fills with seasonal residents and visitors. In the summer the population thins out and the customer mix shifts toward year-round households and the trades that serve them.

Most local websites are built once, for one of those two audiences, and then quietly serve the wrong one for half the year.

Build for the swing instead of ignoring it

A restaurant that needs reservations in February and needs locals in August should not have to rebuild its homepage twice a year. It needs a layout with a section that can change — a promotion band, a featured offer, a hours-and-availability block — without touching the rest of the page.

The same logic applies to a home-services company whose February inquiries come from part-time owners arriving to find a problem, and whose July inquiries come from full-time residents planning ahead. Those two visitors need different reassurance, and a well-structured site can serve both without splitting into two sites.

Keep hours and availability accurate

Seasonal hours are one of the most common sources of bad first impressions in this market. If the site says one thing and the door says another, the review that follows is about trust, not about hours. Whatever system you use, hours should be editable in under a minute by someone who is not a developer.

Plan content around the calendar

Search demand here moves with the season. Questions about rentals, events, maintenance and booking windows surface at predictable points in the year. A site that publishes a handful of genuinely useful pages timed to that calendar tends to age better than one that publishes nothing and then rebuilds every three years.

04Photography Carries More Weight Here Than Copy

In a market with this much design awareness, stock photography is not neutral. It actively signals that the business either has nothing of its own worth showing or did not think it mattered.

Real images of your space, your team, your completed work and your products do more for conversion than another paragraph of description. For visual categories — restaurants, builders, interior designers, event venues, galleries, real estate, marine services — the photography is the argument.

Shoot for the layout, not the album

A common mistake is commissioning a set of beautiful images that do not fit the places the design needs them: a wide hero, a tall mobile crop, a square card, a detail shot. Deciding the layout first and shooting to it produces a site that looks composed rather than assembled.

Show the work, with context

A gallery of unlabeled images is weaker than six projects with a sentence each describing the problem, the scope and the neighborhood. Context is what turns a photograph into evidence. You can see how we present project work in the work section of our own site.

Keep the files disciplined

High-resolution photography and a fast website are not in conflict, but they do require deliberate handling: modern image formats, correct dimensions per breakpoint, lazy loading below the fold. A gorgeous image that arrives three seconds late has already lost the visitor it was meant to impress.

05The Technical Floor: Mobile, Speed and Accessibility

Google uses the mobile version of a site for indexing and ranking, and recommends responsive design as the most maintainable mobile configuration. In practice that means the phone layout is the real website and the desktop layout is the variation, not the other way around.

Google's Core Web Vitals give a reasonable technical baseline: how quickly the largest visible element loads, how quickly the page responds to input, and how much the layout shifts while loading. These are not a complete measure of quality, but a site that fails them is usually failing visitors too.

Accessibility deserves specific attention in Sarasota. This is an older market than the Florida average in many neighborhoods, and a meaningful share of visitors are reading on a phone in bright sunlight. Practical consequences:

  • Body text at a size that does not require zooming, with generous line height
  • Contrast that survives a screen outdoors, not just a monitor indoors
  • Tap targets large enough to hit while standing up
  • Forms with visible labels rather than placeholder text that disappears
  • Phone numbers that dial when tapped, and addresses that open a map

None of this is decorative. Each item removes a reason for someone to give up and call the next listing.

06How to Brief a Web Designer in Sarasota

If you are about to commission a site, the quality of the brief usually predicts the quality of the result more than the size of the budget.

Decide the one thing the site must do

Bookings, estimate requests, phone calls, reservations, applications, gallery inquiries. Write it down before anyone opens a design tool. Everything else — navigation, page count, layout, photography — is downstream of that decision.

Bring real content, or budget for it

Most local website projects stall on content, not design. If you do not have current photography, accurate service descriptions and a clear list of what you sell, the project either waits or ships with filler. Treat copywriting and photography as line items, not afterthoughts.

Ask how the site will be maintained

Who changes the hours. Who adds a project. Who updates a price. If the honest answer is “we call the developer,” the site will be out of date within a year. Frameworks like Framer, which we use for much of our build work, make routine edits genuinely feasible for a business owner, but the structure still has to be planned for it.

Ask what happens to search visibility

Page titles, headings, URL structure, internal links and business information should be handled during the build, not bolted on afterward. Nobody can ethically promise a ranking position — what a well-built site can do is make the business easier for both people and search engines to understand, which is where the probability comes from. We covered the search side in more depth in our guide to local search strategy for Sarasota businesses.
