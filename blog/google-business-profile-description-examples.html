Google Business Profile Description Examples

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01What the Description Field Is For

The description is the short paragraph that appears on your Google Business Profile, the listing people see in Google Search and Maps. Most owners write it once, in a hurry, and forget it. That is a missed chance, because it is one of the few places on the profile where you decide the words yourself.

According to BrightLocal, in a guide last updated in April 2026, business descriptions are limited to 750 characters. Google’s own guidelines say to use the field to provide useful information on the services and products you offer, as well as the mission and history of the business, and to be upfront and honest about it. This post covers what to put in those 750 characters, what to keep out, and four examples by industry.

02Rules Google Sets for the Description

Google’s guidelines for representing your business list what a description must not do. Read them before you write, because an edit that breaks them can be rejected.

  • No links of any type. Google’s page says so directly, and BrightLocal adds that URLs and HTML code should be left out as well.
  • No promotions, prices or sales language. The guidelines give examples such as “Everything on sale, -50%” and “Best bagels in town for $5!” as content to avoid.
  • No low-quality or distracting content, such as misspellings, gimmicky character use or gibberish. BrightLocal also advises against ALL CAPS and stuffing the field with keywords.
  • Nothing irrelevant to the business. Keep it about what you do, who you serve and your history.

These rules make the description a place for plain facts. Save the seasonal offer for a Google post, your website or social media, and keep the description steady.

03Put the Essentials First

Google shows only part of a long description before a “more” link, so the opening matters most. BrightLocal advises putting the most important information in the first 250 characters because descriptions are often cut off after that point. Vendasta, in an article published in October 2026, points out that Google does not publish the exact cutoff and that practitioners estimate it at roughly 244 to 300 characters, so 250 is a sensible guess and not an official number.

In practice, that means your first two sentences should name the business, say what it is, and give the town or service area. Anyone who reads only that much should still know whether you can help them. The remaining characters can hold the extra detail: services, years in business, who you serve and how to reach you.

Compare two openings for the same business. The first reads, “We are a team passionate about excellence in everything we do.” The second reads, “Gulf Breeze Cooling is a family-owned air conditioning company serving Sarasota, Lakewood Ranch and Bradenton.” Only the second tells a stranger what you do and where, and it does so in the characters most likely to be seen. A sentence about passion can come later, if at all, once the useful facts are in place and you have room left over to add a little personality.

If you are a business without a storefront, say clearly which areas you serve, and see our post on Google Business Profile for a business with no storefront for how service areas work.

04A Simple Structure That Works

Most good descriptions follow the same four steps, written as ordinary sentences rather than a list of keywords.

  • Who and where: the business name, what it is, and the town or area.
  • What you offer: the main services or products in plain words, in the order customers ask for them.
  • Why trust you: how long you have operated, who owns it, or a fact that is specific and true.
  • How to act: a short invitation that uses the profile’s own buttons, such as call, message or reserve, without typing a web address.

Vendasta describes a similar five-part approach that starts with the primary category and the services, and BrightLocal’s examples likewise move from name and location to experience, services and a call to action. None of these sources is Google, so treat them as good habits and not as official rules.

A word about search. BrightLocal says that keywords and information in the description do not affect local rankings, though they can help customers decide. No one can guarantee where your profile will appear on Google, so write for the customer reading the page and not for an algorithm.

05Four Examples by Industry

The businesses below are invented to show the structure. Each is under 750 characters, uses no links, prices or offers, and puts the essentials at the start. Change the details to fit your own business and write only what is true for you.

A made-up air conditioning company: “Gulf Breeze Cooling is a family-owned air conditioning company serving Sarasota, Lakewood Ranch and Bradenton. We repair, maintain and replace home cooling systems, and we install duct and thermostat upgrades. Our technicians have worked in Florida homes for more than 15 years, and we answer service calls year-round, including the hot months when systems work hardest. Use the buttons on this profile to call us or request a visit.”

A made-up family dental office: “Siesta Key Family Dental is a general dentistry practice in Sarasota, Florida. We provide checkups, cleanings, fillings, crowns and care for children and adults. Our office has served local families since 2009, and we welcome seasonal residents who need care while they are in town. Our team explains each step before treatment begins. Call or message us through this profile to schedule a visit.”

A made-up neighborhood restaurant: “Harbor Table is a casual seafood restaurant on the Gulf Coast in Venice, Florida. We serve lunch and dinner, with grilled fish, shrimp, salads and a changing daily special. Our dining room and outdoor patio are open for families and groups, and we offer takeout. The restaurant has been owned by the same family since 2012. View the menu and hours on this profile, and call us to reserve a table.”

A made-up boutique: “Coral Lane Boutique is a women’s clothing and gift shop in downtown Bradenton. We carry dresses, tops, jewelry and home gifts from small makers, and we change the selection with each season. Our shop is small, and our team helps customers find sizes and gift ideas. We have been part of the downtown community since 2016. Stop in during open hours, or message us through this profile with a question.”

Notice that each example sounds like a person talking. None says “best” or “number one,” and none repeats the town name six times. The Gulf Coast references appear because they are true for the business, and a business in another market should of course describe its own.

06Checking, Updating and Moving On

After you save the description, read it once on your phone. Check that the first sentence makes sense by itself and that the facts match your website, your hours and your other profiles. Mismatches between your listings can confuse customers more than a plain description ever will, so fix any difference you find in the same sitting.

Review it again when something real changes: a new service, a new owner, a move or a change in your season. A business that caters to winter residents might add that fact before the busy months, and one that closes for hurricane preparations should share closures through the profile’s hours and a Google post, not inside the description.

The description is only one part of the profile. Photos, hours, categories and replies to reviews all do more work for most customers. If you would like a website that matches the profile and gives people more to read once they click, see our services or start a conversation.
