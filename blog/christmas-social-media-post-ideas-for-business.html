Christmas Social Media Post Ideas for Business

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01Four Weeks, and Three Things People Want to Know

Christmas Day falls on a Friday this year, four weeks from the day this post goes up. For a small business that is enough time to do December well on social media, and the season does not ask for anything complicated.

Most lists of Christmas social media post ideas for business run to fifty items, and most of the fifty are decoration: a countdown graphic, a festive quote, a poll about favorite cookies. A customer scrolling in December is usually trying to find out one of three things. Are you open. Can I still get a gift from you in time. Is there a real person behind this account.

So this post is built on three kinds of posts that answer those questions: hours, gifts and thanks. After that come a few local ideas for this part of the Gulf Coast, and a short calendar that takes you from now to New Year’s Day without posting every day.

02Hours: The Least Festive Post Is the Most Useful One

Nobody shares a holiday hours post with a friend. They do act on it. A person deciding whether to drive to your shop on Christmas Eve afternoon needs one fact, and if your account does not give it, a guess or a competitor will.

Make one clear image or plain text post that lists every day that differs from normal, by date and day of the week. For most businesses here that means Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, New Year’s Eve and New Year’s Day, plus any day you close for a staff party or inventory. Write the hours as text in the caption too, since text inside an image is hard to read on a small phone and cannot be read aloud by a screen reader.

  • Post it once in the first half of December and again in the week of Christmas. Many people will see only one of the two.
  • Pin it to the top of your Facebook page and Instagram profile until January 2.
  • Say when you reopen on a normal schedule.
  • If you take appointments, say which is the last day to book before the holiday.

Then make Google agree with the post. According to Google’s Business Profile help page on special hours, you can set special hours when your business temporarily changes its hours or stays closed for up to 6 days in a row, without touching your regular hours. The same page says it is a good idea to confirm your hours for official holidays even when they are the same as usual, because that makes it clear to customers the hours are accurate. In the profile, the path Google describes is Edit profile, then Hours, then Special hours.

Do the website on the same sitting. A line near the top of the home page and on the contact page is enough. The hours on Instagram, Facebook, Google and your site should match to the minute, because a customer who finds two different closing times will trust neither.

03Gifts: Gift Cards and the Last Day to Order

Gift cards deserve more than one mention in December. According to the National Retail Federation’s winter holiday research page, updated November 19, 2026, 47% of holiday shoppers say they would like to receive gift cards this season, ahead of clothing at 44%. The buying side is softer. In a press release of the same date, the NRF said 39% of holiday shoppers plan to give gift cards, down from 43% last year, with planned gift card spending of $26.8 billion, the lowest since 2015. Restaurants were the most popular type at 26%.

Those figures come from a national survey of 7,956 adults conducted for the NRF by Prosper Insights & Analytics from October 30 to November 9. They describe the country, not your own customers, but the gap is worth noticing: more people want a gift card than plan to buy one. A local business that makes its card easy to find and easy to buy is offering something people have said they would like to get.

A gift card post works best when it removes a doubt. Three that do:

  • How to buy it. In the shop, by phone, or online, with the link in your profile. If it can be emailed to the recipient, say so, because that is what a shopper on December 23 needs to hear.
  • What it looks like. A photograph of the real card in its envelope on your counter, or a screenshot of the email version.
  • Who it suits. A massage for a daughter-in-law, a dinner for the neighbors who watched the house all summer, a detailing for a father who will not buy one for himself.

Selling the card on your own site, and the Florida rules on expiration, are covered in our post on gift cards on your website.

The other gift post is the deadline. If you ship anything, tell people the last day to order. For 2026, the Postal Service’s holiday shipping page lists recommended send-by dates for expected delivery before December 25 in the lower 48 states: Thursday, December 17 for USPS Ground Advantage and First-Class Mail, Friday, December 18 for Priority Mail, and Saturday, December 19 for Priority Mail Express. The same page notes that the actual delivery date may vary. Those are the dates a parcel must be mailed, so your own order cutoff has to sit a few days earlier, to leave time to pack.

If you do not ship, you still have deadlines. A bakery has a last day for pie orders, a framer has a last day for custom work, a salon has a last open chair before Christmas Eve. Post each one twice: a week ahead, and the day before.

04Thanks: Say It the Way You Would in Person

The thank-you post is the one owners put off, because the versions they have seen are stiff. A stock photo of ornaments with the words wishing you and yours a joyous season could have come from any business in the country.

A good one sounds like the owner talking at the counter. It is specific, it is short, and it has real people in it.

  • A photograph of the team, in the shop or on the job, in whatever they normally wear. Names in the caption, first names are enough.
  • One true thing about the year. The month the road was torn up and regulars came anyway. The new hire who is now running the front desk. Year five in the same unit.
  • A plain sentence of thanks, written the way you would say it. Thank you for keeping us busy this year is better than any slogan.

Two cautions. Ask before you post a customer’s face or name, even in a happy picture. And leave figures out of it unless they are true and you can stand behind them. A thank-you does not need a number.

05A Gulf Coast December

December here does not look like the pictures on the cards. That is an advantage, because your own photographs will look like nowhere else. Lights wound around a palm trunk, the staff in short sleeves and Santa hats. Winter residents are back, and family is visiting, so some of the people seeing your posts are seeing your business for the first time.

Local events give you a reason to post that is not about selling. According to the City of Sarasota’s events page, the Downtown Sarasota Holiday Parade is on Saturday, December 5, 2026, from 7 to 9 in the evening, on Main Street from U.S. 301 to J.D. Hamel Park, and it is free. WWSB reported on October 22 that this is the 30th annual parade. If you are on or near the route, post your hours for that evening and where to park.

Lakewood Ranch, Venice and other towns nearby hold their own holiday events. Check the date on the organizer’s own page before you mention one, since some are announced late and listings copied from last year are common.

The same visitors will look you up before they come in. Our post on snowbird season prep covers what to check on the website and your profiles for the winter months.

06A Short Calendar From Now to New Year’s Day

This is our suggestion for a business with no one whose job is social media. It comes to seven or eight posts in five weeks.

  • This week: the gift card post, with how to buy it. A decorating photograph if you have one.
  • Week of December 7: holiday hours, pinned. Last order or booking dates.
  • Week of December 14: the deadline reminder, a day before your cutoff. A second gift card post that mentions email delivery, if you offer it.
  • Week of December 21: hours again early in the week. The thank-you post on the 23rd or the morning of the 24th. Then stop.
  • Week of December 28: one post with New Year’s hours and the day you are back to normal.

Between those, short updates such as the tree going up or a tray of orders ready for pickup fit better in Stories than in the feed. Our post on Instagram Stories for business explains how to keep that to a few minutes a day.

A few things can be skipped without loss. Daily countdowns. A discount invented only because other accounts have one. Any trend that has nothing to do with what you do. And posting on Christmas Day itself: schedule the greeting the day before, or let the account rest.

None of this will change where you appear in search results, and no one can guarantee a position on Google. What it will do is make December easier for the people who already want to buy from you.
