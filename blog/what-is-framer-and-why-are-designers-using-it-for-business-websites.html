What Is Framer, and Why Are Designers Using It for Business Websites?

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01What Framer Is, in Plain Terms

Framer is a website builder that runs in a web browser. You design pages on a visual canvas, much like laying out a page in a design program, and then publish them to a live address without writing the underlying code by hand. According to Framer's own homepage, it also includes a content management system, built-in hosting, SEO tools, analytics and A/B testing, along with AI features that can generate and adjust designs on the canvas.

That mix is the main reason designers pay attention to it. A traditional custom site often means a designer draws the pages, a developer builds them and a hosting company runs them, with a handoff at each step. In Framer, the person who designs the page can usually be the person who publishes it, which shortens the path from an approved layout to a live site.

Framer is one tool among many. Others include WordPress, Squarespace, Wix and Webflow, and each has its own strengths. The useful question for a business owner is not which platform is best in the abstract, but which one fits the site you actually need and the person who will look after it.

02Why Designers Like It

The first reason is control over how the page looks. Designers describe the canvas as closer to a design tool than to a template editor, so spacing, type, motion and layout can be set precisely instead of picked from a menu. For a business that cares about a distinct look, that matters more than a long list of plugins.

The second reason is speed. Because design and publishing happen in one place, a change to a headline or a section can go live the same day, and the owner can often preview it first. Framer's Pro plan, according to the pricing page, also includes a staging environment and branching with previews, which let a team try changes before the public sees them.

The third reason is that the finished site does not usually need a stack of add-ons to run. Hosting, the CMS and basic analytics sit inside the same account, so there are fewer separate logins and fewer pieces that can fall out of date. That does not make the site maintenance-free. Someone still has to keep the content current, which is the part many owners underestimate.

None of this means Framer produces a better result on its own. A site with a muddled message looks muddled in any tool. For help deciding what belongs on the page, see our guide to what should be on a small business homepage.

03What It Costs, According to Framer

Pricing is where this topic changes most often, so treat the figures below as a snapshot from Framer's pricing page as read on October 7, 2026, and check the page again before you decide. Framer prices each site separately, and the plans are billed per site rather than per account.

According to that page, the plans break down like this:

  • Free: $0, with a Framer-provided domain, 1 GB of bandwidth and 1,000 pages.
  • Basic: $10 per month, with 50 GB of bandwidth, 2 CMS collections, 1,000 CMS items and 30 pages.
  • Pro: $30 per month, with 100 GB of bandwidth, 10 CMS collections, 2,500 CMS items and 150 pages.
  • Enterprise: custom pricing, with limits and security features set by agreement.

The comparison site Siter, which says it read the figures from Framer's pages on September 3, 2026, notes that the $10 and $30 prices are the annual-billing rates, that month-to-month billing costs more, and that extra editors cost $20 per month on Framer's pricing page. It also notes that Framer has changed its pricing more than once, which is another reason to confirm the current numbers.

Keep in mind that the subscription is only one part of what a website costs. The monthly platform fee does not include design, writing, photography, setup or ongoing updates, and scope is what drives the total. 04Who Framer Suits, and Who It May Not

Framer tends to fit a business whose website is mainly a presentation of the business. Think of a design studio in Sarasota, a boutique hotel on the Gulf Coast, a law or accounting practice, a restaurant with a menu that changes a few times a year, or a contractor showing finished projects. These sites have a handful of pages, a clear call to action and content that does not change daily.

It can also suit a business that expects to add articles, project pages or team profiles over time, because the built-in CMS handles that kind of content. Just note the limits. On the Basic plan, according to Framer's page, you get 2 CMS collections and 30 pages, which a small service business may never exceed but a large catalog will.

It may be a weaker fit when the website is mostly a store, a booking engine or a members area. Those jobs usually call for a platform built around them, such as a dedicated e-commerce or scheduling system, and it is common to connect one to a simple marketing site instead of forcing everything into one tool. A seasonal business, such as a boat charter near Anna Maria Island, might keep a clean Framer site for the story and photos and link out to a booking tool for the calendar.

Ask also who will make the edits. Framer is friendlier to non-technical editors than many systems, but it is still a design tool. An owner who wants to change a price at 9 p.m. may be happier with a simpler editor, or with a designer on call for updates.

05Limits to Know Before You Commit

The most important limit is portability. According to Brix Templates, which publishes guidance on Framer, Framer does not offer HTML export for self-hosting, so a site built there stays tied to the platform. If you later want to leave, you would normally rebuild the site elsewhere, and the content and design come with you only in the sense that you can copy them across by hand.

The same source points to a few other constraints. It says bandwidth beyond a plan's allowance is billed as an add-on, that localization into extra languages is an additional per-language fee, and that Framer cannot be used for health information subject to HIPAA. A medical or dental practice that handles patient information should ask about this before choosing any website tool. Those are one reviewer's findings, so confirm anything that matters to you in Framer's own documentation.

Search visibility deserves a straight answer. Framer includes SEO tools, but no tool can guarantee a position on Google, and the platform you choose is only one factor among content, reputation and the competition in your area. If your site is already live and you are wondering why it is not appearing, read why a new website may not show up on Google.

Finally, think about vendor dependence the way you would about any monthly subscription. If the company changes its plans, which it has done before according to Siter, your cost changes with it. That is not a reason to avoid Framer. It is a reason to know what you are signing up for and to keep a copy of your own text and photos.

06How to Decide

Start with the job the website has to do. Write down what a customer should be able to do in the first minute: call, request a quote, read a menu, see your work. If a presentation-style site covers it, Framer is a reasonable candidate. If the site is mainly a store or a booking system, look at tools built for that.

Then ask any designer you are considering why they recommend a given platform. A good answer names your needs, such as how often you will update the site, who does the editing and how much you plan to grow. A weak answer is a habit.
