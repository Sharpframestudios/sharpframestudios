Professional Email on Your Own Domain: Google Workspace or Microsoft 365?

By Sharp Frame Studios · Back to the journal ↗

01The Case for Email on Your Own Domain

An address like yourname@yourbusiness.com tells a customer that a real business is on the other end. An address ending in a free consumer mailbox service can look like a side project, and some people hesitate before sending a deposit or personal details to it. Your own domain also gives you control, because the address stays yours if you ever change providers.

The domain and the mailbox are two separate things. The domain is the name you register, such as the one your website uses. The mailbox is the service that stores and sends your mail, and that is what you rent from a provider each month. Most small businesses choose between two: Google Workspace and Microsoft 365.

This post compares the two for a small business owner, covers what the published plans include, and walks through a sensible setup order. Prices change often, so treat any number here as a snapshot and confirm it on the provider’s own page before you buy.

02What Each One Is

Google Workspace is Google’s business version of Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Docs and Meet. You sign in through a browser or a phone app, and everything runs online. If you already live in Google Docs and Google Calendar, this will feel familiar.

Microsoft 365 Business is Microsoft’s business bundle built around Outlook, Teams, OneDrive and the Word, Excel and PowerPoint family. If your accountant, your insurance agent or a larger client sends you Word and Excel files all day, that familiarity can matter. Some of the plans include the full desktop apps, and some do not.

Both are well established and both let you use your own domain for email. Neither is the wrong answer for a small business, which is why the choice usually comes down to the tools you already use and a few plan details.

03What the Plans Include and Cost

On pricing, start with the caution that neither company sets one number for everyone. Google’s pricing page shows annual and monthly billing options, and says on that page that choosing annual billing with a one-year commitment saves 16 percent. The dollar amounts per user did not display in the text we could read, so check the current figure on Google’s page directly. Google also offers a 14-day trial on that page.

Storage is the clearest difference on the Google side. According to Google’s pricing page, Business Starter includes 30 GB of pooled storage per user, Business Standard includes 2 TB and Business Plus includes 5 TB. Custom business email is part of the lineup.

For Microsoft, the compare page lists Business Basic at $7.00 per user per month when paid yearly, on an annual subscription that renews automatically. Office Watch reported in July 2026 that Microsoft raised business prices effective July 1, with Basic moving from $6 to $7 and Standard from $12.50 to $14, and Premium staying at $22. Office Watch is a news site rather than Microsoft, so treat the Standard and Premium figures as reported rather than official.

The plan detail that surprises people is on Microsoft’s side. According to Microsoft’s compare page, Business Basic includes web and mobile versions of Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook, along with 1 TB of cloud storage per user and a 100 GB primary mailbox. The desktop versions of the apps come with Business Standard. If you want to open the Word installed on your laptop, Basic is not enough.

04How to Choose

A few practical questions settle most cases. You do not need a perfect answer to each one:

  • Which tools do your clients and partners send you? If most files arrive as Word and Excel documents, Microsoft 365 reduces friction. If you share links to documents and work in a browser, Google Workspace fits easily.
  • Do you want the desktop apps? A bookkeeper or contractor who works on large spreadsheets may want Microsoft Standard rather than Basic. Someone who mostly writes emails and uses a calendar may never notice.
  • How many people need mailboxes? Both are priced per user, so a team of three costs three times one person. Count the seats that need a real mailbox, and consider whether shared addresses such as info@ or office@ can route to an existing user.
  • Where does your phone and calendar live today? Switching providers is possible but takes effort, so staying close to what your phone already uses saves time.
  • Who handles support? If someone in your family or a local IT person already knows one platform, that is worth more than a small price difference.

A solo owner with a simple need often does well on the cheapest paid plan of either provider. Consider upgrading when you hit a real limit, such as storage or a missing desktop app, instead of buying the largest plan up front.

05The Order to Set It Up In

The order matters more than the provider, and doing it out of sequence is how people end up locked out of their own mail. This is the sequence that tends to go smoothly:

  • Register the domain in your own name, using an account you control. Our guide to where to buy and keep your business domain covers the choices and the renewal habits that prevent trouble.
  • Pick the provider and start the trial or the first plan. Create your own account as the administrator, using a personal email address as the recovery contact.
  • Prove to the provider that you own the domain. This usually means adding a short verification record in your domain’s settings.
  • Add the mail records the provider gives you. These point the domain’s mail to Google or Microsoft. Until they are in place, mail sent to your new address will not arrive.
  • Create the mailboxes, then test by sending messages to and from an outside address.
  • Turn on two-step sign-in for every account, and set up the extra sender-checking records your provider recommends so your messages are less likely to land in spam.
  • Only then switch your website contact form, business cards and Google Business Profile to the new address.

If your website and email both use the same domain, take care when changing website hosting later. The mail records live with the domain’s settings, and a careless change to them can silently stop your mail. When Sharp Frame Studios or anyone else moves a site, the mail records should be listed and checked before anything is changed.

06Mistakes That Cost Small Businesses

The most common problem is registering the domain through a freelancer or an agency, with their login instead of yours. When the relationship ends, the domain and the mail can be hard to recover. Always make sure you are the owner on the account.

The second is one shared login. If a whole team signs in as the same user, you lose a clear record of who did what and you cannot remove one person without changing everything. A paid seat for each person is usually cheaper than the confusion.

The third is forgetting the seasonal pattern many Sarasota-area businesses have. If you serve snowbirds, you may rely on email more heavily from October through April, and customers who are back up north will write to you at odd hours. A mailbox that goes quiet because an annual plan lapsed in the summer can cost you a season’s worth of enquiries. Put the renewal date in your calendar.

Finally, remember that even with a professional address, no one can guarantee that every message lands in the inbox. Setting up the sender-checking records and avoiding spammy wording help, but spam filters make their own decisions.
